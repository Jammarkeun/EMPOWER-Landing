Problem & Objectives

Careful records that never connect

CDE Manpower Services is a manpower agency in Sta. Cruz, Laguna. It recruits workers and supplies them to client companies. Each set of records is kept carefully on its own, but nothing joins them.

Current situation

  • Applicant records sit in physical folders.
  • Employee details live across Excel files and Google Sheets.
  • Records of workplace violations are kept apart from both.
  • Folders are moved by hand, and a misfiled folder makes a qualified applicant invisible.
  • The choice of whom to send is made from an officer's memory and judgment, and the reasoning is never written down.

Why it matters

When a client company asks for ten production helpers, someone has to go through the folders by hand to find out who is available and whose documents are complete.

When that client asks why these five were sent and not five others, or an applicant asks why they were not chosen, there is no record to point to. A searchable database would fix the first problem but not the second. That is why EMPOWER is a decision support system: it gathers the information, applies rules a person defined, shows its reasoning, and leaves the decision to a person.

Objectives

To develop a web-based system that keeps each worker's recruitment, deployment, and employment record in one place for CDE Manpower Services, and supports explainable deployment decisions.

Out of scope by design: payroll and client company logins.

  1. Centralize applicant, employee, disciplinary, and separation records in one system.
  2. Derive the Folder 1, 2, and 3 filing automatically from verified documents.
  3. Provide explainable, criteria-based ranking of applicants for each manpower request.
  4. Enforce CDE's recruitment and deployment sequence on the server.
  5. Give coordinators, applicants, and employees secure, role-limited self-service.

Methodology

Built in iterations with the agency

EMPOWER was developed using an Agile / Rapid Application Development approach from June to November 2026, with consultations with CDE and panel recommendations folded back into each iteration.

  1. PlanningJun 1 to Jul 15

    Scope and system objectives set with CDE Manpower Services.

  2. RequirementsJul 16 to Aug 15

    Consultations and data checklists with the agency, documenting its folders, forms, and process.

  3. DesignJul 20 to Aug 31

    Database schema, REST API contract, screen wireframes, and the applicant lifecycle map.

  4. DevelopmentAug 1 to Sep 15

    Laravel API and React interface built module by module.

  5. TestingSep 16 to Oct 20

    Automated backend and frontend suites, with continuous integration across SQLite, MariaDB, and PostgreSQL.

  6. DeploymentOct 21 to Nov 30

    Supabase for the final defense, with XAMPP MariaDB for local demonstrations.

Evaluation

System testing covers the end-to-end workflows, role-based access, report exports, and the audit trail. The user evaluation looks at ease of use, attitude, intention to use, and system quality, with ISO/IEC 25010 as its basis.

System Features

One record, six stages

An applicant cannot go from "Applied" straight to "Deployed", because that path does not exist. Each stage is dated and attributed to the person who recorded it.

  1. 1

    Application

    Walk-in or online registration, confirmed in person.

  2. 2

    Screening

    Staff check the applicant's identity against their documents.

  3. 3

    Requirements

    Documents verified, and the folder updates itself.

  4. 4

    Training

    A recorded completion is needed before endorsement.

  5. 5

    Client evaluation

    HR endorses, and the client's decision is recorded.

  6. 6

    Deployed

    The applicant becomes an employee and keeps their history.

Decision Support

A ranking anyone can check

Scoring is rule-based, not AI. Every point traces back to a weight an HR officer entered and a value on the applicant's record, so a recommendation can be defended to a client or to an applicant who was not selected.

  1. HR sets the criteria

    For each request, HR chooses what counts and how much each factor is worth.

  2. Gates pass or fail

    Mandatory rules such as age or height add no points. Failing one means the applicant is not recommended.

  3. Scored criteria earn points

    Education, experience, certifications, skills, availability, distance, communication, and reliability.

  4. The percentage becomes a band

    Highly recommended from 85%, recommended from 70%, and reserve pool from 50%. An administrator can change the cut-offs.

  5. A person decides

    The system recommends. It never hires. Deployment stays a separate act by HR.
